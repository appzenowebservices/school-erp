To build an AI-native education consultant for a Class 10 Physics lesson on Optics, your platform needs a dual-engine architecture: one that acts as a Pedagogical Co-Pilot for the tutor and a Hyper-Personalised Guide for the student.
Here is the strategic framework designed around the Indian curriculum (NCERT/CBSE) for Class 10 Optics (Reflection and Refraction).
------------------------------
## 1. The Core AI Engine Framework (The Knowledge Graph)
Before looking at the user interfaces, your AI backend must break down "Optics" into a granular Concept Map & Competency Matrix rather than just chapters.

* Granular Concepts: Spherical Mirrors $\rightarrow$ Ray Diagrams $\rightarrow$ Mirror Formula $\rightarrow$ Magnification $\rightarrow$ Refraction $\rightarrow$ Lens Formula $\rightarrow$ Power of Lens.
* Taxonomy Levels: Define every concept across three levels: Recall (definitions), Application (ray diagrams), and Analytical (numerical problems and sign conventions).

------------------------------
## 2. The Tutor Perspective: The "Pedagogical Co-Pilot"
From the tutor's side, the AI should not replace the teacher but remove their administrative and diagnostic friction.
## 📋 Pre-Class: Automated Diagnostic & Lesson Planning

* Gap Analysis: The AI reviews the students' past performance data (e.g., if they struggled with Linear Equations in Math, the AI flags that they will likely struggle with the Mirror Formula algebra).
* Dynamic Resource Generation: The AI generates localized, real-world analogies. Example: "Use the curve of a shiny steel spoon to explain concave and convex surfaces before teaching focal lengths."

## ⏱️ In-Class: Real-Time Socratic Prompts

* Socratic Copilot: Instead of giving answers, the AI provides the tutor with a "Hint Flow Diagram" to guide struggling students.
* Misconception Alerts: The AI flags common Indian classroom pitfalls to the teacher ahead of time (e.g., "Students frequently mess up the negative sign for virtual image distance ($v$) in the mirror formula. Remind them of the Cartesian sign convention graph").

## 📝 Post-Class: Automated Evaluation & Homework Personalization

* AI Grader: The tutor uploads photos of handwritten ray diagrams. The AI reads the image, detects missing arrowheads (a classic mark-loser in CBSE), and marks it instantly.
* Divergent Homework: The AI clusters the batch and creates three distinct, curriculum-aligned worksheets based on class performance.

------------------------------
## 3. The Student Perspective: The "Hyper-Personalised Guide"
From the student's side, the AI must feel conversational, visual, and infinitely patient.
## 🔍 Interactive Concept Immersion

* Multimodal Translation: If a student fails to understand the definition of "Refraction", the AI shifts medium instantly: Text $\rightarrow$ Dynamic Ray-Tracing Simulation (e.g., air to glass) $\rightarrow$ Vernacular Video Explainer.
* Contextual Hook: Relates physics to things they care about (e.g., "How does your smartphone camera use a combination of lenses to zoom in without losing focus?").

## 🧭 Real-Time Scaffolding (The "Anti-Cheating" Engine)

* Guided Problem Solving: When a student faces a numerical question (e.g., Find the position of an object placed 10cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15cm), the AI blocks the final answer. It forces a step-by-step dialogue:
1. "First, let's identify the mirror. Is it concave or convex?"
   2. "Great. Now, according to the sign convention, should the focal length ($f$) be positive or negative?"
   3. "Perfect. Now plug it into the Mirror Formula."

## 📈 Gamified Mastery & Retention

* Spaced Repetition Flashcards: Optics has many rules (e.g., position of image when object is at $C$). The AI serves quick 2-minute visual quizzes right before the student forgets them.
* Exam-Alignment Mode: As the CBSE Board Exam approaches, the AI shifts tone from "Conceptual" to "Strategic", teaching the student exact keyword patterns needed to score full marks in 3-mark and 5-mark questions.

------------------------------
