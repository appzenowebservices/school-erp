1. **(1 mark · Easy)** State the two laws of reflection of light. `[PHY-10-01 · OBJ1]`
2. **(1 mark · Easy)** Define the refractive index of a medium. `[PHY-10-01 · OBJ3]`

10. **(4 marks · Hard)** An object 5 cm tall is placed 30 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find (a) the image distance, (b) the magnification, (c) the height of the image, and (d) the nature of the image. `[PHY-10-01 · OBJ2]`

**1. (1 mark)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- Law 1: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal. *(½)*
- Law 2: the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane. *(½)*

**2. (1 mark)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- The refractive index of a medium is the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed of light in that medium: n = c / v. *(1)*
- (Equivalent form: n = sin i / sin r.)

**3. (1 mark)** — *chapter node PHY-10-01, misconceptions list*
- **R = 2f**, i.e. f = R / 2, for a spherical mirror of small aperture. *(1)*
- Note the common error: f = R is wrong. *(chapter misconception)*

**4. (1 mark)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:1`
- One dioptre is the power of a lens of focal length 1 metre; P = 1 / f with f in metres. *(½)*
- SI unit of the power of a lens = dioptre (D). *(½)*

**5. (2 marks)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- With the tooth between the pole and the focus, a concave mirror forms a virtual, erect and magnified image. *(1)*
- The dentist therefore sees a large, upright image of the tooth — which is why a concave mirror is suitable. *(1)*

**6. (2 marks)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- (a) Since n = c / v, a larger n means light travels slower in that medium. Water (1.33) → about c/1.33; glass (1.50) → about c/1.50, so light is slower in glass than in water. *(1)*
- (b) Glass has the higher refractive index (1.50 > 1.33), so glass is optically denser than water. *(1)*

**7. (2 marks)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. *(1)*
- Sign convention: distances are measured from the pole along the principal axis; distances measured in the direction of the incident light are positive and those opposite to it are negative. u is negative; for a concave mirror f is negative, for a convex mirror f is positive. *(1)*

**8. (2 marks)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:1`
- f = 25 cm = 0.25 m; P = 1 / f (f in metres). *(1)*
- P = 1 / 0.25 = **+4 D** (positive because a convex lens is converging). *(1)*

**9. (4 marks)** — `doc_4a563fc9eb3b:0`
- (a) u = −20 cm, f = −15 cm (concave mirror). Using 1/v + 1/u = 1/f:
  1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/20 = (−4 + 3)/60 = −1/60. *(correct formula + substitution — 1)*
  → **v = −60 cm**: the image forms 60 cm in front of the mirror, on the same side as the object. *(correct value + position — 1)*
- (b) m = −v/u = −(−60)/(−20) = **−3**. *(1)*
- (c) v and m both negative → the image is **real, inverted and 3 times magnified**. *(1)*

**10. (4 marks)** — **newly written numerical; key derived and verified here (not lifted from the doc — no doc citation)**
- (a) u = −30 cm, f = +10 cm (convex lens). Using 1/v − 1/u = 1/f:
  1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/10 − 1/30 = (3 − 1)/30 = 2/30 → **v = +15 cm** (on the far side of the lens). *(1)*
- (b) m = v/u = 15 / (−30) = **−0.5**. *(1)*
- (c) h′ = m × h = (−0.5) × 5 = −2.5 → the image is **2.5 cm tall** (inverted). *(1)*
- (d) v positive and m negative with |m| < 1 → the image is **real, inverted and diminished** (half the object's height). *(1)*
